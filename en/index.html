Home becomes a surface that serves.

Tesselle covers floors, walls and ceilings with tiles that plug in as they click on. Light, display, sound, air, heat: move the tile and its function moves with it.

Technology moved on. Our homes didn’t.

A socket, a switch, a ceiling light: where they were fifty years ago. The smart home became a pile of gadgets.

A cage carries power and data.

It is designed to assemble inside the room, with no tools and no drilling. 48 V and the network reach every node of the grid.

Tiles click on.

Ceramic, stone, wood, textile. Tiles sit edge to edge and are replaced one by one.

Every tile takes a role.

Eighteen functions go where they serve: radar above the bed, rain in the shower, a weighing floor, air in the kitchen.

Furniture plugs in too.

The table comes down from the ceiling. The coffee table draws power through its magnetic feet. The bed sits in the floor.

And the home lives with you.

It follows the day, tells the cat from you, watches at night and turns everything off when you leave.

Your data stays home

  • Recognised tiles A tile that cannot prove its identity is never powered.
  • Processed on site Nothing raw leaves. The rest is designed to go through a single filtered gate, with your consent.
  • Physical cut-off Microphones and radar are designed with a hardware switch and an indicator software cannot drive.
  • Life safety kept apart Smoke, CO and gas have their own circuit, with no shared software.

Frequent questions

What is Tesselle?

Tesselle is a modular living-surface project: a structure set inside the room carries 48 V power and data, and 500 mm tiles click onto it across floors, walls and ceilings. Each tile can become light, display, speaker, charger, sensor, heater or purifier.

Can I buy Tesselle today?

No. The project is at the design stage: circuit boards routed, firmware compiling, parts modelled. The first physical tile remains to be built and measured.

How much does an equipped apartment cost?

For the 58 m² reference apartment, the order of magnitude is €150,000 as a prototype and €56,000 in large series, ± 30%, excluding tax, installation and margin. These are working assumptions: the real bill of materials is not costed.

Do walls need drilling?

No, that is the principle of the system: the cage is designed to stand inside the room without drilling, and tiles go on without tools. Large ceiling spans remain to be validated. Anything involving water or 230 V goes through certified teams.

Are there cameras?

Not by default. Presence comes from a 60 GHz radar, a 32 × 24 pixel thermal array and floor load sensors, with no identifiable image.
